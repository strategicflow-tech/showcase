What this pass covers

  • Subject and first line. Checks approximate mobile truncation, labels, emphasis, urgency, and familiar sender-first openings.
  • CTA and links. Counts distinct actions and flags empty targets, generic labels, HTTP links, and common shorteners.
  • Reader focus and readability. Compares sender and reader pronouns, counts words, estimates reading time, and finds long sentences or paragraphs.
  • Facts and wording. Shows the full sentence around prices, percentages, dates, durations, figures, and absolute claims so you can check the source.
  • Draft cleanup. Looks for unresolved personalization tokens, filler, repeated words, and several questions in one message.
  • HTML and images. Checks image alt text and explicit dimensions, and estimates image markup compared with text.
  • Marketing email reminders. Looks for an unsubscribe path and postal address. Rules vary; this is a checklist, not legal advice.

Copy checks run locally. A pattern flag is not a verdict, factual claims are not verified, and this is not an inbox placement or deliverability test.

FAQ

What this check can and can’t tell you.

What does the pre-send checker check?

It checks extracted HTTP(S) links for reachable, redirected, broken, timed-out, blocked, or error responses; flags common spam-risk wording patterns; extracts numbers, prices, percentages, durations, dates, and selected claims for human verification; and, for HTML, estimates image-to-text balance and notes when a separate text alternative was not provided. These checks do not verify factual truth, run an inbox placement test, or include email-client previews.

What else does the local email review check?

Alongside links and spam-risk wording, the browser reviews subject length and mobile truncation risk, generic first lines, CTA count and wording, sender-to-reader pronouns, readability, unresolved personalization and draft markers, repeated words and questions, HTML image alt text and dimensions, marketing unsubscribe and postal-address clues, and numbers or absolute claims that need a source check. The subject and body stay in your browser. These checks do not provide a deliverability score.

Is this pre-send email checker truly free?

Yes, $0 and no account. Each visitor gets 10 free checks per week.

How is this different from Email on Acid, Litmus, or Mailgun Inspect?

This free checker covers only the checks listed on this page and does not provide email-client previews or the Decision Friction Model audit. Mailgun Inspect Premium is listed at $199 per month after the first free month and includes link validation and spam content checks; its Basic plan is $99 per month after the first free month and does not include those checks. Litmus pricing is not published on its pricing page, which requires contacting sales. Prices as listed on their official pricing pages, 23 Nov 2026.

Is my email stored or sent to a server?

The subject and email body are analyzed locally in your browser and are not sent or stored by this checker. Only HTTP(S) links extracted from the email are sent to the link-check endpoint. The destination websites may receive ordinary link-check requests.

Does this checker guarantee deliverability?

No. Spam-risk patterns are simple local signals, not a deliverability prediction or guarantee. Inbox placement also depends on factors this tool does not test, including sender reputation, authentication, recipient engagement, and mailbox-provider decisions.

What are facts-to-verify?

They are numbers and claim patterns that may deserve a human source check, including prices, percentages, durations, dates, and wording such as only, #1, or free for X days. The tool extracts them for review; it does not verify whether any claim is true.

Can I upload an email file?

Paste the email or upload an .html, .txt or .eml file. Files are read in your browser and not uploaded. The .htm extension is also accepted, up to 2 MB. For multipart .eml files, the HTML body is preferred and a text/plain alternative is noted when present.
