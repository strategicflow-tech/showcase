How the Strategic Flow Audit works

The audit is a behavioral diagnostic, not a style review. It checks whether the architecture of your email removes decision friction at each stage — before the reader reaches the CTA.

Strategic Flow — key terms

The audit uses specific vocabulary to name failure patterns precisely. These terms appear in every diagnostic report. Knowing them makes the fix obvious.

Decision Friction
Any architectural element that interrupts, delays, or eliminates the reader's decision to act.
Not a tone problem. A structural problem. The 7-point audit maps where friction appears.
Filing Label Subject
A subject line that announces the email topic like a folder tab instead of naming a consequence.
"New Feature: Dashboard Update" is a label. "Your reports now load 4x faster" is a signal.
Feature-First Bias
Email leads with what the product does instead of what the reader gains.
The reader's brain filters for relevance to their situation, not product capability.
Consequence Signal
A subject line or hook that names a specific outcome or cost the reader recognizes as their own.
Creates open rate lift without urgency tricks or manufactured scarcity.
Architecture vs Copy
Copy is word choice and tone. Architecture is the structural sequence of information and decisions.
Rewriting copy without fixing architecture produces the same result with different words.
Activation Gap
The missing consequence between a completed step and the next action in an onboarding sequence.
Most onboarding emails tell users what to do but not what they lose by waiting.
