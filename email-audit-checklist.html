01
Subject Line
Does it create a curiosity gap or name a specific consequence? Or does it announce the topic like a folder label?
Filing Label Subject
Consequence signal present
Filing Label
"New Feature: Dashboard Update"
Consequence Signal
"Your reports now load 4x faster"
Same email. Different architecture. The second one names what changes for the reader — not what the product did.
02
Lead Construction
Does the first sentence name a consequence, outcome, or reader problem? Or does it open with context, a caveat, or "we are excited to announce"?
Consequence-After-Caveat
Lead names reader outcome
Caveat First
"We are excited to announce our most powerful update yet..."
Consequence First
"If you have ever refreshed a dashboard three times waiting for data — that problem is gone."
The reader's brain filters for relevance in the first 3 words. Lead with what changes — not who built it.
03
Feature-to-Outcome Translation
Does the body name what changes for the reader? Or does it describe what the product does without saying what the reader gains?
Feature-First Bias
Reader outcome named
Feature-First
"Advanced reporting module now supports real-time data sync."
Outcome-First
"Stop refreshing. Your dashboard updates automatically — no manual sync, no stale numbers."
Features describe what the product does. Outcomes describe what the reader gets. Only one drives clicks.
04
Visual Hierarchy
Does the most important claim appear above the fold? Or does everything look equally important with no clear priority?
Missing Hierarchy
Clear visual priority set
Missing Hierarchy
12 features listed at equal size. Reader doesn't know where to look.
Clear Hierarchy
One dominant claim above fold. Supporting details below. The eye has a path.
When everything is equally important, nothing is. Give the reader's eye a direction.
05
Before / After Contrast
Is the transformation explicit? Does the email name what the reader's situation looked like before and what changes after?
No Transformation Named
Transformation made explicit
No Contrast
"Our new reporting feature is now live. Check it out."
Transformation Named
"Before: manual refresh every 10 minutes. After: live data, always current. Nothing to configure."
Invisible transformation creates no urgency. Name where the reader is now and where they land after clicking.
06
Social Proof
Is there a third-party voice — a named customer, a specific result, a number — or is every claim made by the brand about itself?
Zero Social Proof
Third-party voice present
Self-Validated
"Our most powerful feature yet. Trusted by thousands of teams."
Third-Party Voice
"Cut reporting time from 40 minutes to under 5. — Sarah K., Head of Analytics at Growthco"
Brands that validate themselves are dismissed. One specific customer result outperforms any self-reported claim.
07
CTA Language
Does the CTA name what the reader owns or accomplishes? Or does it use guest language — "Learn more", "Click here", "Explore"?
Generic Urgency Theatre
Ownership language used
Guest Language
"Learn more" / "Explore features" / "Click here"
Ownership Language
"See my live dashboard" / "Fix my reporting" / "Get live data now"
"Learn more" describes what the brand offers. Ownership language describes what the reader gains. Only one drives action.
