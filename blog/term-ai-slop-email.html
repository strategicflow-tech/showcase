What Is AI Slop Email?

AI Slop Email is generic, unedited AI-generated email copy with interchangeable claims and no specific proof.

Definition

AI Slop Email
defined.

AI Slop Email is lifecycle or outreach copy generated by an AI model and shipped without structural editing: identifiable by generic hedge phrases, claims any competitor could also make, and missing specific proof or consequence. It is not about who drafted it. Human-written copy can be AI Slop Email too if it is equally generic.

The tell is interchangeability. If the same paragraph could sit in a competitor's onboarding sequence without anyone noticing, it is not carrying enough product-specific evidence. Generic language makes the message easy to summarise and easy to ignore.

The structural fix is consequence-first editing. If you want to see which checkpoint is failing in a real email, run the free diagnostic. Teams that need a deeper rebuild can review the Pro plans.

What changes
Replace the generic claim with the one specific mechanism, number, or consequence unique to this product and this reader. Run the copy through the free audit before sending it, then use the Pro plans when you need the full structural review.
FAQ

Questions people
ask about it.

Is all AI-generated email copy AI Slop Email?+
No. The term names a pattern of genericness and missing proof, not the drafting tool. AI-generated copy can be specific and evidence-led after structural editing, while human-written copy can still be AI Slop Email when it relies on interchangeable claims and no concrete consequence.
How is this different from Feature-First Bias?+
Feature-First Bias is about lead structure: the email starts with what the product shipped instead of what changed for the reader. AI Slop Email is about generic language and absent proof regardless of whether the structure is feature-first. The two patterns can appear together, but they diagnose different failures.
Can a human write AI Slop Email?+
Yes. A human can write AI Slop Email whenever the message uses generic capability claims, hedge phrases, and no product-specific proof or reader consequence. The pattern is visible in the copy, not in the authoring tool.
